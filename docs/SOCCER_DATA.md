# Soccer comparison — sources and scope notes

The five-club table is illustrative. It is not a natural experiment, a causal
test, or a like-for-like accounting comparison. The clubs were chosen to span
the two models named in the brief: academy-led / cost-conscious clubs
(Nordsjælland, Bodø/Glimt, Viking, and Barcelona's La Masia) and star-name
spending in the doldrums (Manchester United). Barcelona is a hybrid: a famous
academy and a top-three European wage bill, so it does not sit cleanly on one
side.

Season window: Norwegian clubs use the 2024 calendar season; Barcelona,
Manchester United, and Nordsjælland use 2024–25. This mismatch is stated in the
table.

## What the table claims

- The named clubs differ by an order of magnitude in wage bill.
- The academy-led / cost-conscious clubs are competitive within their leagues
  at a fraction of the star-name club's wage bill.
- Barcelona is a hybrid, and accounting scopes differ, so the table does not
  identify a causal effect of wage discipline on performance.

## What the table does not claim

- That low wages caused Bodø/Glimt's title, Viking's third place, or
  Nordsjælland's fifth place.
- That Manchester United's 15th-place finish was caused by its wage bill.
- That the NFL salary-cap results are the same mechanism as the soccer
  outcomes. The soccer table is a qualitative mirror, not a statistical test.
