# Reception scan: the practical operator and the values reader

**Status:** the non-empirical annex to the [adversarial audit](README.md). It
does not modify the judged artifacts or the public summaries.

This is a scan of two reader postures, not a personality test. "ESTP" is
shorthand for the **practical operator**: a coach, scout, or cap manager who
wants a decision, a cadence, and a number that fits the cap sheet. "ENFP" is
shorthand for the **values reader**: someone who cares about the people in the
model, the distribution of costs, and what the work is doing to the sport.

Both readers can dismiss the work in under a minute. The scan records what they
would say, what lands, and the cheapest fix that does not require new data.

## 1. Practical operator

| # | Objection | What lands | Cheapest fix | Residual |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "What do I do Monday? Who owns it, how often, and when do I stop?" | Tier 3 says "monthly" in one place and "4–6 weeks" in another; there is no pilot. | A one-page operating memo: inputs, the review list, the 4–6 week cadence, the owner (coach + analytics), the stop rule, and the cost range. Label it a hypothesis. | Untested |
| 2 | "The dollar does not fit my cap sheet." | APY is not cap hit or cash; the haircut, cost, and adoption are judgments; there are no signing dates. | The dual totals and the expected-role ladder; an APY/cap/cash table; name the cost-bearer; stop saying "saving." | No cash model |
| 3 | "I do not have 10 Hz, NGS, or OTC data." | The signal claims depend on tracking data. | A data-availability table; the minimum inputs (snap share, draft, public production); mark which claims require tracking. | Some claims need data a club lacks |
| 4 | "It tells me to bench named players." | The player-level surplus and role outputs can be read as "should play more." | No public player-level rankings; aggregate review lists; substitution, not addition; a load-management caveat; no health claim. | Internal use still has distributive effects |
| 5 | "It will not survive scheme, health, assignment difficulty, or a coaching change." | Role is prior snap share; the performance measure omits assignment, trust, and health. | Narrow the definition; list the unmeasured confounders; keep the non-causal label; add a role-definition sensitivity if cheap. | Identification unresolved |

**Response card.** "This is a review trigger, not a verdict. The model flags a
short list of contracts and roles where pay and playing time diverge from
measured production. Run the review for two cycles; if the list does not
survive film and the coach's judgment, discard it."

## 2. Values reader

| # | Objection | What lands | Cheapest fix | Residual |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "This is a pay-cut model; the money moves from players to owners." | The finding can be cited as a management saving; the loser is named, but the symmetry is buried. | Say "transfer," not "saving"; state that agents can use the same coefficient; name the loser in the same paragraph; drop "politically manageable"; no policy recommendation. | One-sided citation risk remains |
| 2 | "It is surveillance and commodification of bodies." | The Combine is a biometric exam; the repo has no consent or ethics note. | A data-ethics note; no new collection; frame the finding as a validity check on existing data; no expansion recommendation; no player-level biometric output. | The Combine remains a biometric institution |
| 3 | "It racializes sorting and builds a caste system." | The analysis is race-blind; the draft-status finding invites the frame. | An explicit race-blind statement; no demographic inference; no "caste," "suppression," or "status-legible" in public summaries; no player-level underuse lists. | The frame can still be supplied externally |
| 4 | "It optimizes violence; more snaps mean more collisions." | Pressure and sacks are collision outcomes; role reallocation can add exposure. | Substitution, not addition; a load-management caveat; no health benefit; no "should play more" lists. | No injury data |
| 5 | "It is betting fuel and an insider edge." | Role and usage estimates are prop-denominated; the player-level CSVs exist. | No public player-level outputs; an explicit no-betting-use rule; avoid "edge," "mispricing," and "inefficiency"; no week-to-week forecast. | Aggregate numbers remain useful to bettors |

**Response card.** "The work does not say any player is overpaid, and it is not
a verdict on any person. It says the market prices a role construct in a way
this model does not fully explain. The same coefficient can be used by a player
or agent to argue that role is underpriced. The dollar is a transfer, not a
social saving."

## 3. What both readers are right about

- **The work is not a decision tool yet.** There is no pilot, no cadence test,
  and no cash model. The practical objection is fair.
- **The work has a distributive cost.** The contract route reprices the
  incumbent player. The values objection is fair.
- **The work is not a forecast.** It is a cross-sectional screen. Both readers
  should be told this before the number.
- **The work cannot settle identification.** Assignment difficulty, health,
  trust, and expected role are unmeasured. Neither a better slide nor a better
  paragraph fixes that.

## 4. What would make each reader dismiss the work

| Reader | The 30-second dismissal |
|---|---|
| Practical operator | "This is an academic regression with a consulting deck attached; it does not tell me which contract to reprice or what to do when the coach disagrees." |
| Values reader | "This is a cold model that turns players into line items and gives owners a pay-cut argument." |

The fixes above reduce the surface area. They do not eliminate either
dismissal, because both contain a true premise: the work is a screen, and the
contract route has a loser.
