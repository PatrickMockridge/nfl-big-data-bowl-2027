# Co-optation and insider threat: how the work is read from outside

**Status:** the non-empirical annex to the [adversarial audit](README.md). It
does not modify the judged artifacts or the public summaries.

## Purpose and limits

This annex records two things the statistical audit cannot:

1. How external critics may read the findings as support for a deeper
   indictment of the sport.
2. Why people inside the sport may resist the work regardless of its technical
   merit.

It is not an empirical audit. It does not endorse the readings. It separates
what a critic gets right from where a critic overreaches, because both matter:
the parts that are right are the parts that cannot be defused.

The annex names critique traditions, not organizations or individuals. The
readings are steelmanned; a weak version would be easy to dismiss and useless
to plan against.

## 1. The mechanism

**The work is a Rorschach.** The same numbers support opposite conclusions. The
allocation gap says late picks are underused; that is a pro-player finding. The
role premium says clubs pay for role beyond performance; that is a
cost-cutting finding. Each side can cite half and ignore the other. A reader
who wants to know what the work "really" says will find what they are looking
for.

**Rigor increases quotability.** The more precise the number, the more useful it
is to a critic. "About $6M a year" is a better slogan than "the association is
positive with a wide confidence interval." The caveats protect the author
academically; they do not protect the author politically.

**Caveats do not survive citation.** A headline is a compression. The
compression drops the sample, the CI, the tier, and the counterexamples. The
repo's own messaging guides say "not a single number without the distribution"
and "not 'players are overpaid.'" Those rules bind the author, not the critic.

**The both-sides problem.** Labor critics cite the allocation gap and the
draft-caste reading. Management critics cite the role premium and the
cost-saving reading. Each side attacks the half it dislikes. The author looks
either naive or opportunistic, and no single constituency owns the work.

**The unsayable problem.** Insiders may privately agree with parts of the
analysis. The work makes the private critique public and quantified. They can no
longer say "that's just opinion"; there is now a number. The rational response
is not to amplify it.

**The prestige-transfer problem.** A league-branded competition awarding the
paper transfers legitimacy to the critique. Even a judge who likes the work may
vote no for brand reasons.

**The procedural veto.** Because insiders cannot rebut the statistics without
engaging the frame, they use procedure: not responsive to the brief, external
data, post-deadline changes, speculative dollars, "not actionable for coaches."
The technical merit is never evaluated. This is the cleanest rejection, and it
is the one to expect.

## 2. Six readings

### 2.1 Labor / monopsony

**The tradition.** The NFL is a cartel and a monopsony. The draft, the rookie
wage scale, non-guaranteed contracts, and short careers transfer value from
players to owners. The union's central fights are over the split, guarantees,
and eligibility.

**The findings cited.** The allocation gap (+0.59 SD for late picks and UDFAs,
−0.92 SD for early picks); the role premium ($7.05M uncut / $1.82M capture net
for a median team); the implementation cost and adoption language; the
"savings" framing.

**The steelman.** The league's own data shows that playing time — the gateway
to pay, starts, and career length — is allocated by draft status, not
performance. A late pick has to outperform his role by 0.59 SD to keep his job;
an early pick can underperform by 0.92 SD and keep his. The league's analysts
then model the resulting pay gap as a saving. This is a cartel that controls
entry through the draft, controls opportunity through the depth chart, and now
has a pricing model to justify paying less for both.

**What it gets right.** The draft does allocate opportunity beyond the rookie
contract. Role is a gateway to pay. The short-run dollar in the model is a
transfer from players to clubs, not a social saving. The implementation-cost
and adoption language is one-sided: the cost is borne by the analyst, the
saving by the player.

**Where it overreaches.** The model is an association, not proof of intent or
collusion. The market may be paying for expected role, health, or unmeasured
productivity. The work itself says that if every team adopts the principle, the
market reprices and the surplus flows to players
(`implications/writeups/01_valuation.md:105`). The model is symmetric in
principle: an agent can use the same coefficient to argue that role is
underpriced. The analysis does not test a labor-market hypothesis.

**Who feels it.** The league office, owners, and GMs (the pay-cut reading);
coaches (the allocation reading); the union, which would use the allocation
finding and attack the role premium.

**What would reduce the co-optation.** Publish the symmetry. State the
cost-bearer in the same paragraph as the dollar. Replace "savings" with
"transfer" where that is what is meant. Do not lead with the implementation
cost as if the only cost were the analyst's.

### 2.2 Racialized sorting and the draft caste

**The tradition.** The NFL's scouting and draft pipeline is racialized. The
Combine's body measurement and the language of "athleticism" have a history.
Roster demographics and coaching/front-office demographics do not match. The
draft is a sorting institution, not just a hiring market.

**The findings cited.** The 10 Hz Combine tracking; the burst; the draft-caste
allocation gap; the phrase "status-legible."

**The steelman.** The league measures young men's bodies at 10 Hz before they
have earned a dollar, and this paper shows the measurement predicts who gets
pressure and who gets paid. It also shows that players drafted late perform
above their role. The league has quantified its own caste system and called it
an allocation gap.

**What it gets right.** The Combine is a body-measurement institution. The draft
structures opportunity beyond the rookie contract. The analysis is race-blind
and therefore cannot rebut the mapping with its own data. The direction of the
allocation finding is critical of the league's underuse of late picks, which is
the part a critic will emphasize.

**Where it overreaches.** The analysis contains no race variable. The direction
of the finding is not evidence of a racial effect; it is evidence of a
draft-status effect. The Combine finding is about a task-specific movement
skill, not a racial trait. The critic supplies the demographic composition; the
repo cannot confirm or deny it. The work does not use "caste" or "racial"
language, but the finding invites the frame.

**Who feels it.** The league office, clubs, and scouts. The Combine is a league
institution, and the draft is a core labor-market institution.

**What would reduce the co-optation.** State explicitly that the analysis is
race-blind and cannot support a racial inference. Do not publish player-level
"underused" lists. Avoid "caste," "suppression," and "status-legible" in public
summaries. Do not imply that the draft's only function is sorting.

### 2.3 Biometric surveillance and athlete data

**The tradition.** Athletes are data subjects. Wearables and tracking create a
surveillance regime with unclear consent, compensation, and control. The Combine
is a pre-employment biometric exam for unpaid workers.

**The findings cited.** The 10 Hz tracking; the burst; the 510-player Combine
cohort; the recommendation to use the signal.

**The steelman.** The Combine is a biometric exam for unpaid workers. This paper
treats 10 Hz body tracking as a legitimate productivity signal and recommends
using it. The lesson is that the more closely you surveil a body, the more
accurately you can price it. That is the league's entire data regime, stated
without embarrassment.

**What it gets right.** The Combine is a pre-employment biometric exam. The repo
has no consent, data-rights, or ethics section. The finding normalizes the data
regime and recommends using more of it.

**Where it overreaches.** The data are league-provided and contractually
consented. The paper does not collect new data. The finding is about
task-specific movement in a football drill, not invasive biometrics. The
recommendation is to use existing data better, not to collect more.

**Who feels it.** The league office, players, and the union.

**What would reduce the co-optation.** Add a data-ethics and consent note.
Frame the finding as a validity check on existing data, not a case for more
collection. Do not recommend expanding biometric measurement.

### 2.4 Player safety and the commodification of violence

**The tradition.** Football is a collision sport with a documented brain-injury
problem. Analytics that optimize for pressure and sacks optimize for violence.
The Combine is a violence-selection mechanism.

**The findings cited.** Burst → pressure → snaps; the allocation logic that the
explosive linemen should play more; the role premium as a way to reallocate
snaps.

**The steelman.** The paper identifies the most explosive bodies and recommends
exposing them to more collisions. The league's analytics is a
violence-optimization function.

**What it gets right.** Pressure and sacks are collision outcomes. More snaps
mean more exposure. The work has no load-management or injury analysis, and the
allocation recommendation does not distinguish substitution from addition.

**Where it overreaches.** The paper does not recommend more snaps for any named
player. It recommends reallocating role, which can be a substitution. The injury
link is not measured. The work is about evaluation, not load management.

**Who feels it.** The league's health and safety staff, coaches, and players.

**What would reduce the co-optation.** Add a load-management caveat. Frame role
reallocation as substitution, not addition. Do not publish player-level "should
play more" lists. Do not claim a health benefit.

### 2.5 Betting and integrity

**The tradition.** The league has sold its product to sportsbooks while claiming
integrity. Player props are role-denominated. Tracking data and analytics are
the raw material of betting edges. Insider information is a integrity risk.

**The findings cited.** Player-level role, surplus, and usage estimates; the
language of "mispricing," "premium," and "inefficiency"; the allocation gap as a
market inefficiency; the player-level CSVs.

**The steelman.** The league's tracking data, its Combine, and its analytics
competition are a pipeline for the gambling industry it partners with. The role
premium is a prop-betting signal with a citation. The player-level surplus files
are a list of who should play more, which is exactly what a prop bettor wants.

**What it gets right.** The output is a usage/role forecaster. Props are
role-denominated: targets, receptions, sacks, tackles. The repo uses the
vocabulary of market inefficiency. It has no betting safeguards and no
statement against betting use. The player-level artifacts exist, even if they
are gitignored.

**Where it overreaches.** The repo contains no betting content, no odds, no
public player-level rankings, and no forecasting model. The estimates are
cross-sectional associations, not a week-to-week projection. The work is not
sold to bettors.

**Who feels it.** The league's integrity staff, clubs, and players.

**What would reduce the co-optation.** Keep player-level outputs out of public
artifacts. Avoid "edge," "mispricing," and "inefficiency" in public summaries.
Add an explicit no-betting-use citation rule. Do not publish a ranked
"should play more" list.

### 2.6 Meritocracy and antitrust

**The tradition.** Sport sells itself as a meritocracy. The draft and the cap
are cartel institutions defended as competitive balance. The restraint of trade
does not end at the rookie contract.

**The findings cited.** The depth-chart null; the allocation gap; the cap-pays-
performance result.

**The steelman.** The league sells itself as a meritocracy. Its own data says
the depth chart doesn't reward performance. The draft's restraint of trade
doesn't end at the rookie contract; it structures the career. The cap pays
performance, but management allocates opportunity.

**What it gets right.** The draft does allocate opportunity. The depth-chart
null is a hit on the meritocracy myth. The cap result relocates the critique to
management, not the market.

**Where it overreaches.** The performance measure is incomplete; the null is
absence of evidence; the draft may be justified by competitive balance; the
paper does not test an antitrust claim. The cap result is consistent with a
meritocratic market.

**Who feels it.** The league office, owners, and coaches.

**What would reduce the co-optation.** Distinguish "the model does not detect"
from "the league does not reward." Avoid "caste" and "suppression." Do not
present a descriptive gap as a legal or policy finding.

## 3. Insider-threat mechanics

**Identity threat.** Coaches' professional judgment is the product being
evaluated. "The depth chart does not reward measured performance" says their
core skill is status-driven, even with the absence-of-evidence caveat. A judge
who has spent a career in a football building is being told the building's
central allocation decision is not performance-based.

**Institutional threat.** The work touches the draft, the Combine, the cap, and
the depth chart. These are the league's four labor institutions. A finding that
implicates all four is not a normal analytics entry.

**Legal and CBA threat.** The numbers are citable in bargaining, litigation, and
public advocacy. A league-branded competition awarding them transfers prestige
to the critique. The league office has reasons to care beyond the paper's
accuracy.

**Deniability loss.** Insiders may privately agree. The number makes the private
critique public and quantified. They can no longer say "that's just opinion."
The rational response is not to amplify it.

**Asymmetric risk for the judge.** Rejecting a good paper costs a judge almost
nothing. Endorsing a radioactive one costs them internally. The incentive is
one-sided, and it does not depend on the paper's quality.

**The procedural veto.** Because insiders cannot rebut the statistics without
engaging the frame, they use procedure: not responsive to the brief, external
data, post-deadline changes, speculative dollars, "not actionable for coaches."
The technical merit is never evaluated. This is the rejection to expect.

**Prestige transfer.** A league-branded competition awarding the paper transfers
legitimacy to the critique. Even a judge who likes the work may vote no for
brand reasons.

**Internal reform versus external indictment.** Insiders can tolerate "we can do
better." They cannot tolerate "the league is a caste system." The work's
language is mostly internal and technical, which helps. The numbers are the
indictment; the frame is not.

## 4. What can and cannot be defused

**Can be defused, partly:**

- **Asymmetry.** Publish the player-side use of the model (role is underpriced;
  agents can negotiate on it) alongside the club-side use. This removes the
  "pay-cut blueprint" as the only reading.
- **Cost-bearer disclosure.** State in the same paragraph that the dollar is a
  transfer from players to clubs, not a social saving.
- **Tier labels.** Keep the measured/descriptive/inferred separation in every
  public summary, not only in the writeups.
- **Citation rules.** Publish what the work may and may not be used for.
- **No player-level public lists.** Keep the ranked surplus and "should play
  more" artifacts out of public view.
- **No named executives.** Remove the team-fit section's named adopters.
- **No "savings" language.** Use "transfer," "repricing," or "counterfactual."
- **No "caste," "suppression," or "status-legible" in public summaries.**

**Cannot be defused:**

- The allocation finding is a critique of the depth chart. It says the
  allocation decision does not detect performance. That is the finding.
- The Combine is body measurement. The finding validates a biometric signal.
- The role premium is a short-run pay-cut model. The model's action is to pay
  less for the same role.
- The player-level outputs are betting-adjacent in shape, even without betting
  content.
- The work makes a private critique citable. That is the deepest threat, and it
  is a consequence of doing the work well.

**Irreducible.** The work is dangerous to insiders precisely because it is
useful to outsiders. The same number that makes a general manager curious makes
a critic eloquent. That is not a bug in the analysis; it is the price of
crossing from football analytics into political economy.

## 5. Citation rules

These rules are for anyone citing the work, including the author.

1. **Cite the tier.** Measured association, descriptive decomposition,
   model-reproduced, inference, or normative claim. Do not promote a lower tier
   to a higher one.
2. **Cite the sample and the CI.** n=306 for the 2026 APY gap; n=909 for the
   cap ledger; n=233 for the role null; n=64 for the burst-pressure model.
3. **Do not cite a dollar without the distribution and the low/conservative
   cases.** The median is not the mean; the top decile carries half the premium.
4. **Do not say "does not reward performance" without "detectably."** The
   measured claim is a null, not a positive finding.
5. **Do not say "players are overpaid."** The residual is a deviation from a
   model, not a verdict.
6. **Do not cite the work for betting.** The estimates are cross-sectional and
   are not a forecasting model. Player-level outputs are not public artifacts.
7. **Do not cite the work as causal.** Free agents are selected; team changes
   are not random; role is endogenous.
8. **Do not name executives as adopters.** The team fits are a reading, not
   evidence of adoption.
9. **Do not publish a player-level "should play more" list.** The strict tail is
   a review list of 13 player-seasons, not a population.
10. **State the uncut versus capturable distinction.** The $7.05M is the uncut
    annual role premium; the capturable amount at the base 0.40 haircut is
    about $2.8M gross / $1.8M net.
11. **State the transfer-versus-saving distinction.** The dollar is a
    counterfactual transfer, not observed cash.
12. **Do not use "caste," "suppression," or "status-legible" in a public
    summary.** They are interpretations, not measurements.
